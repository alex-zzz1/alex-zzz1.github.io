Baptism

Fiction individualism story for ENG289 at UofT. What animal do you picture when reading?

The colony moved as one. Their eusocially connected brains thought only of the collective, without any notion of the individual. What need is there? A macroscopic network of nodes, their brains worked together like a server room of computers connected by pheromones.
An expansive society had been dug out, and a huge population cultivated. Nowhere was their collective-never-individual ethos more apparent than their mating ritual: workers consummate their ultimate purpose by reproducing with the Queen, exhausting their nutritional resources and rupturing their insides with the violent nature of copulation, sacrificing themselves for the colony, for themselves.
We moved as an oblong mass through uneven terrain, light rainfall barely impeding our straight path.
The smell of blood focused our trek on a worthy objective. We found a giant laid on the forest floor. Fleshy, hairy in patches, and with only four legs and a torso ripped open by predators, it was a completely different creature from us. Its head was large in comparison to its body. Maybe it was intelligent as we are.
We each tore off tissue to carry back as the rain turned heavy. One of us approached a cliff of mangled flesh overlooking a red-and-blue organ with a deep gash that pulsated weakly every few seconds.
Best not to harvest anything still moving.
A massive raindrop squarely struck that worker, forcing a topple over the edge into the organ’s opening. Panic pheromones escaped, but nothing else. An insignificant casualty in the face of such nourishing loot.
The worker flailed, beginning to drown in the creature’s blood and be crushed by the organ’s throbbing, releasing distress pheromones in a frenzy. Finally his claws caught hold of flesh and he moved toward the light. As he freed himself from the red lake, he recognized that the others were gone: he was alone. In an unfamiliar predicament for a worker, he followed his instincts and tore off a piece of the beating organ, heading back towards the colony.
Night had fallen. Forced by injury and exhaustion, the worker set the flesh down in order to rest. The thought of consuming it to recuperate and lighten his load so he could make it back and continue working curiously floated into his mind. He had only ever eaten universally distributed rations within the colony.
As he ate, he watched the full moon glow bright yellow, reflecting an orange glow off of the gory scene.
I arrived back at the colony as the mating ritual was nearing its beginning, feeling born anew from my rest and refueling. I joined a single file line of workers navigating through the compact dark tunnels, guided by the olfactory language of the comrade in front of me, who himself was guided by the one in front of him and on and on until the Queen’s chamber. The largest room in the colony was doused in Her regal pheromones, triggering visceral hormones that beckoned me forward. To my duty. To my fate. To the ecstasy that is to die for the colony in the Queen’s embrace. But a sight I had beheld plenty in the past this time caught my heart like a hook.
The Queen rested upon a mountain of dead workers and drones. The morbid scene made me doubt my march towards my dedicated doom. I thought of the joy of consuming fresh meat. The sensation of the world’s textures under my claws during exploration. The beauty of the moon. Pheromones of primal fear and existential anguish spilled out of me uncontrollably as I agonized over something none other of my kind had ever thought before: I wanted, to live. More than I wanted to serve my deepest biological purpose.
“?!!!!!” My glands screamed.
“?...” I felt with my antennae, the confused signal from my comrades filling the air.
As I reached the front of the line and the base of the deathly mountain, my legs shook as my steps slowed to a stop. I calmed myself as my final decision revealed itself in my mind. I turned and scurried, bursting with every ounce of my energy whence I came, frantically and forcefully stepping over my comrades.
“Traitor?” As I neared the light, their pheromones went from confusion to this clear signal of accusation.
They began attacking me, striking my legs and biting at my head and abdomen as I sprinted past, filled with an unfamiliar kind of determination as I sustained more and more damage.
As I exited the colony, injured and tailed by a murder of lethal, efficient workers, my pheromones no longer signaled fear nor confusion nor any simple communicative byte shared with a hundred others, but rather something more complex that brewed in my heart and mine only.
I was free!
